LEE'S PLAN

A better approach to Boulder roads.

Safety matters. So do clarity, traffic flow, public trust, and whether a project actually works after it is built.

01

Use Real Data

Evaluate projects using local crash data, close calls, traffic impacts, and post-construction results.

02

Keep Streets Clear & Intuitive

Prioritize visibility, understandable intersections, reasonable speeds, and designs people can navigate naturally.

03

Listen Before Building

Require meaningful neighborhood input and stronger City Council oversight before major road changes move forward.

01

Pause and re-evaluate

Pause feasibility, engineering work, and grant proposals for new major road projects until they are reevaluated against goals that balance safety and traffic efficiency.

02

Reconsider Baseline

Reevaluate protected intersections from 32nd to Mohawk using local crash data, real-world traffic performance, and direct observation.

03

Revisit bus-only lanes

Review recent bus-only lane conversions and whether they improve transit enough to justify added congestion or confusion.

04

Simplify Baseline east of Foothills

Lee supports restoring two through lanes in each direction with a straight-through bike lane and removing unnecessary bollards.

05

Make left turns clearer

Use simple green, yellow, and red left-turn arrows as the default and avoid flashing yellow arrows during a WALK signal where conflicts can occur.

06

Bring back the data

Reactivate and expand Boulder's Crash Dashboard and create a close-call reporting system so future projects can be evaluated with better evidence.

WHY HE'S RUNNING

Independent review. Better questions. More accountability.

Lee is a longtime Boulder resident who has lived near Baseline for decades. He attended CU and earned degrees in electrical engineering, with additional experience in statistics, reliability, and failure analysis.

He believes Boulder can do a better job evaluating major transportation projects before, during, and after construction.

Where “No Confidence” fits

Lee's campaign began with a simple conclusion: he has lost confidence in the way Boulder plans and implements major road projects. He is running to offer voters a more transparent, data-driven alternative.

WHAT LEE WANTS TO CHANGE

Transportation policy should work in the real world.

Lee's campaign focuses on project design, oversight, transparency, and measurable results.

01

Baseline Project

Lee argues that parts of the project introduced complexity and congestion without enough evidence that the redesign addressed the highest-priority safety problems.

02

Protected Intersections

Lee wants Boulder to evaluate whether these designs fit local conditions instead of assuming one approach is automatically right everywhere.

03

Bus-Only Lanes

Lee wants a clearer accounting of whether lane conversions materially improve transit service and how they affect everyone else using the corridor.

04

Vision Zero

Lee supports the goal of safer streets while arguing that Boulder should be more rigorous about measuring whether its chosen strategies are actually reducing severe crashes.

05

Public Oversight

Lee believes City Council should critically review major transportation projects rather than simply defer to department expertise.

06

Simple, Clear Design

Lee favors excellent visibility, straightforward intersections, clear markings, reasonable speeds, and less unnecessary visual clutter.

2
bicycle accidents

In Lee's review of seven years of Boulder Crash Dashboard data, he found two bicycle accidents between 32nd Street and Mohawk, the stretch where multiple side-street intersections were redesigned.

A BASELINE CASE STUDY

Start with the problem, then choose the solution.

Lee says he reviewed bicycle crash data because bicycle safety was a major rationale for the Baseline Project. His analysis led him to question whether the redesign focused on the locations with the greatest documented crash history.

His broader point is simple: before changing a major corridor, Boulder should understand what is happening, where it is happening, and how success will be measured afterward.
